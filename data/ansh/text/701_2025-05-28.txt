          decreasing within the next 3 years by about $600,000. Sunsetting local income tax in
          2028 will be eliminated, which is what paid for the “trolly” service which is now known as
          the connector. Without being able to source funds to run this route, it may need to be
          eliminated in 2027. Leadership at the State House is not interested in increasing other
          taxes and the likelihood of us running standalone local income tax is very low. This
          means that by going into 2026 our shortfall has increased. There is also a possibility of
          PMTF funding decreasing for the next 2(two) fiscal years with the possibility of taking
          back some 2025 dollars.                                                   
                                                                                    
          SB-1 did grant cities to run their own income tax citywide which does open up a few
          different plans that Mr. Smith is working hard to figure out how to get a sustainable
                                                                                    
          income package for CityBus.

                                    Guiding Objectives                            
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
    The COA planning process incorporated research into existing demographic and socio-economic conditions, route utilization
    analysis and public engagement activities. Route recommendations are based on the following foundational objectives:
      Realign routes to provide more direct rides to destinations. CityBus operates on a pulse system with nearly all routes
                                                                                  
      ending at the CityBus Center (CBC) on a pulse schedule that allows passengers to transfer to other routes, as needed.
      The analysis questioned if the pulse system is still the most effective way to serve the community, given the locations of
      trip generators, current travel patterns of the local residents, and planned or projected land use changes in Greater
                                                                                  
      Lafayette.                                                                  
      Maintain or improve route frequency. CityBus strives to maintain 30-minute frequencies or improve to even more
      frequent service, especially during peak hours of operation, for as many routes as possible. Frequent service improves
                                                                                  
      convenience for the riders.                                                 
      Promote balanced service. CityBus has examined the demographic and socio-economic conditions of the community
      and its riders. The transit system strives to provide equitable service for everyone in the service area. All recommended
                                                                                  
      changes considered equity impacts on minorities/non-minorities, people of all ages, and individuals with disabilities.
      Maintain service affordability. CityBus has not increased fares for over 20 years even as operating expenses have
      inflated. The system is considering a new fare structure that will help CityBus close the gap between operating expenses
                                                                                  
      and revenues. The new structure includes new fare pass options and a pay-as-you-go fare capping option that will
      benefit frequent riders.                                                    
      Operate within available and sustainable financial resources. All recommended service change options consider the
                                                                                  
      rider first and foremost. CityBus has carefully examined the financial reality and has determined that service changes
      will involve reductions in span of service at least until such time that additional revenues can be identified. With all
      changes, CityBus strives to maintain the best service for the most people within a sustainable budget.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
    The recommendations include service improvements that respond to the rider's needs and are based on cost reduction and
    cost control strategies. These strategies aim to reduce expenses by minimizing unnecessary or inefficient route segments
                                                                                  
    and replacing them with more efficient routes. CityBus must consider the cost reduction and control approach unless
    opportunities for additional revenue improve.                                 
                                                                                  
                                  Span of Hours Changes                           
   Under the cost-reduction scenario, CityBus must reduce its span of hours. This option is presented with significant
   reluctance; however, it is the most impactful way to sustain the most service overall. Under this scenario, CityBus will
                                                                                  
   continue operating its existing span of weekday and Saturday hours, but will eliminate Sunday service on all routes. In 2024,
   CityBus operated eight routes on Sundays.                                      
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
   Elimination of Sunday service in combination with the route alignment changes in Option 1 reduces total revenue hours by
   nearly 16 percent and reduces operating expenses by approximately 17.21 percent. In Option 2, these changes save 18% to
   32% and put CityBus in a sustainable financial position. In 2024, Sunday service provided approximately 70,850 trips. Most
                                                                                  
   of those trips will be lost because of the reduced span of hours; however, some passengers may be able to complete their
   Sunday trips on other days of the week. Average trips per hour of operation on Sundays, by route, ranges from 45 to
   551.By comparison, average trips per hour of operation on weekdays, by route, ranges from 171 to 3,706.

             Option 2: A Reimagined Network to Streamline Routes and Improve Productivity Exhibit 1
                                                                                  
  Option 2 takes a fresh approach to the entire CityBus network by implementing multiple transfer points strategically located
  across the service area and connecting the transfer points with routes that transport passengers within their neighborhood or
  offer a quick transfer to anywhere in the service area. The Option 2 scenarios are summarized in the tables and text that follows.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
  The cost-control aspects of Option 2 include designing a combination of “core routes” and “local routes.”
    The core routes (depicted as solid lines) serve the areas with the highest density of people and popular destinations and
                                                                                  
    connect to multiple transfer points, including the CityBus Center (CBC), with a high frequency of service.
    Local routes (depicted as dashed lines) add coverage and connect neighborhoods and transfer points.
    The combination of core and local routes with multiple transfer points will reduce travel time for the rider and promote
                                                                                  
    more direct access from point to point with less frequent need to travel downtown for a transfer. Transfer points include:
       CityBus Center                                                             
       Walmart West                                                               
                                                                                  
       Walmart East                                                               
       Ivy Tech                                                                   
       Pay Less (Beck Ln.)                                                        
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
  Cost-reduction aspects of Option 2 include reducing the span of hours on weekday evenings to and Saturdays to 6:00 PM or 9:00
  PM and eliminating Sunday service. The reduced span of hours is balanced by direct routes with increased frequency of service
                                                                                  
  to every 15 or 20 minutes on core routes with 30-minute frequencies on local routes, and/or offering peak/off-peak hours for
  core routes and local routes.

                CEO  REPORT    TO  GLPTC   BOARD    OF DIRECTORS                    
                                                                                    
                              MEETING DATE: May 28, 2025                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          CEO                                                                       
          Senate Bill 1 has passed, and our preliminary analysis of the law is that we will see a
          reduction of ~$600,000 in property tax revenue over the next three years. We also have
          an agreement with the Cities and County to receive ~$900,000/year in Local Income
          Tax (LIT) funds. That agreement will now end as of 2028. We are still projecting a
          budget shortfall for 2026 (if we do not make any service cuts) of $3M, which is now
                                                                                    
          ~$3.2M. The fare changes should help offset this. This will also inform the choices we
          make with the COA. We were able to get the state law changed for CDL operators so
          starting July 1, 2025, we can hire and train 18–20-year-olds as bus operators.
          The hydrogen fueling station will begin construction this summer, with completion
          planned to coincide with delivery of the fuel cell buses.                 
          The APTA Legislative conference was informative. We were able to hear from both the
          FTA Acting Administrator Tariq Bokhari and from Secretary of Transportation Sean
          Duffy in person. They were both excited about the possibilities that transit provides.
          We have extended invitations to both Senator Young and Banks and Congressman
          Baird to visit our facility, or we could meet them at a district office. We made
          connections with staff at all three offices and will be able to request letters of support
          from each for grant applications.                                         
          DOT announced funding opportunities for the 2025 Low/No Program and Bus/Bus
          Facilities Program. These are the same ones we received grants from for the hydrogen
          fuel cell project. The also announced funding opportunity for a Regional Investment
          Accelerator using the TIFIA loan program. We are reaching out to INDOT to see if they
                                                                                    
          are applying, and how we might partner with them on projects for our area.
                                                                                    
          COO                                                                       
          May has been an energizing and collaborative month across the organization as we
          continue preparing for major changes and building momentum in key areas.  
                                                                                    
          CityBus was proud to sponsor and participate in Greater Lafayette Commerce’s 2025
          Diversity Summit, which brought together local and national voices, including keynote
          speaker Kwame Christian. He did a presentation on how to have difficult conversations
          to promote inclusion and equity throughout our community.                 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          The COA Steering Committee met to review potential route network design options and
          is preparing a recommendation for Board consideration. Conversations with 
          stakeholders about transfer points in a future network are ongoing, with an emphasis on
          ensuring locations are functional in the short term while also supporting potential future
          transit-oriented development (TOD).                                       
                                                                                    
          We reached a major milestone with the approval of our interlocal agreement with Carroll
          County, allowing CityVan vanpooling services to operate in all contiguous surrounding

          Our community engagement process around the proposed fare structure is well
          underway, with a mix of virtual and in-person meetings, along with a public survey.
          Simultaneously, department heads and key staff have been participating in multiple
          train-the-trainer sessions with Masabi in preparation for the upcoming launch of our
          EZFare JustRide system.                                                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          We continue to explore innovative workforce development efforts. Several department
          heads met with local and international representatives from ATU to discuss how to best
          launch the operator mentorship program. The goal is to improve recruitment, retention,

          Finally, I recently shared a message that will become a larger part of our operations

          transition and beyond.
