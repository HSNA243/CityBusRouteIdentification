                                    Guiding Objectives                            
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
    The COA planning process incorporated research into existing demographic and socio-economic conditions, route utilization
    analysis and public engagement activities. Route recommendations are based on the following foundational objectives:
      Realign routes to provide more direct rides to destinations. CityBus operates on a pulse system with nearly all routes
                                                                                  
      ending at the CityBus Center (CBC) on a pulse schedule that allows passengers to transfer to other routes, as needed.
      The analysis questioned if the pulse system is still the most effective way to serve the community, given the locations of
      trip generators, current travel patterns of the local residents, and planned or projected land use changes in Greater
                                                                                  
      Lafayette.                                                                  
      Maintain or improve route frequency. CityBus strives to maintain 30-minute frequencies or improve to even more
      frequent service, especially during peak hours of operation, for as many routes as possible. Frequent service improves
                                                                                  
      convenience for the riders.                                                 
      Promote balanced service. CityBus has examined the demographic and socio-economic conditions of the community
      and its riders. The transit system strives to provide equitable service for everyone in the service area. All recommended
                                                                                  
      changes considered equity impacts on minorities/non-minorities, people of all ages, and individuals with disabilities.
      Maintain service affordability. CityBus has not increased fares for over 20 years even as operating expenses have
      inflated. The system is considering a new fare structure that will help CityBus close the gap between operating expenses
                                                                                  
      and revenues. The new structure includes new fare pass options and a pay-as-you-go fare capping option that will
      benefit frequent riders.                                                    
      Operate within available and sustainable financial resources. All recommended service change options consider the
                                                                                  
      rider first and foremost. CityBus has carefully examined the financial reality and has determined that service changes
      will involve reductions in span of service at least until such time that additional revenues can be identified. With all
      changes, CityBus strives to maintain the best service for the most people within a sustainable budget.
                                                                                  
                                                                                  
    The recommendations include service improvements that respond to the rider's needs and are based on cost reduction and
    cost control strategies. These strategies aim to reduce expenses by minimizing unnecessary or inefficient route segments
                                                                                  
    and replacing them with more efficient routes. CityBus must consider the cost reduction and control approach unless
    opportunities for additional revenue improve.

             A Reimagined Network to Streamline Routes and Improve Productivity   
                                                                         Exhibit 1
  The recommended option takes a fresh approach to the entire CityBus network by implementing multiple transfer points
  strategically located across the service area and connecting the transfer points with routes that transport passengers within
                                                                                  
  their neighborhood or offer a quick transfer to anywhere in the service area. The option has been presented to the public.
                                                                                  
  The reimagined network includes designing a combination of “core routes” and “local routes.”
                                                                                  
    The core routes (depicted below as solid lines) serve the areas with the highest density of people and popular destinations
    and connect to multiple transfer points, including the CityBus Center (CBC), with a high frequency of service.
    Local routes (depicted below as dashed lines) add coverage and connect neighborhoods and transfer points.
                                                                                  
    The combination of core and local routes with multiple transfer points will reduce travel time for the rider and promote
    more direct access from point to point with less frequent need to travel downtown for a transfer. Transfer points include:

       Walmart West                                                               
       Walmart East                                                               
       Ivy Tech

       Statement of Work                                                            
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
       CTE will use its Zero-Emission Smart Deployment Methodology to guide this project, which is
       designed to help agencies understand ZEB technologies and how to deploy them successfully. This
       Statement of Work (SOW) describes the phases of the project and the specific tasks CTE will
       complete to support the Greater Lafayette Public Transportation Corporation (CityBus) in the
                                                                                    
       deployment of four (4) New Flyer fuel cell electric buses (FCEBs) and supporting hydrogen fueling
       and hydrogen bus maintenance infrastructure for its 2024 Low-No project. This SOW provides an
       overview of activities and an estimation of the time to complete each phase of the project. Each
       phase overview includes a description of the services to be provided by CTE with the associated
       CTE deliverables, fees, and payment terms. This SOW also includes a description of CTE’s project
       controls and risk management procedures to guide the effective and efficient management of this
       project. A summary of the phases, CTE milestones, and fees can be found at the end of this SOW.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
       CTE has already performed extensive work with and for CityBus to support the deployment of
       FCEBs and supporting hydrogen fueling and hydrogen bus maintenance infrastructure under the
       agency’s 2023 Low-No project. CTE’s SOW for this project was developed to supplement and
       expand the work of the 2023 Project. Accordingly, several CTE-standard project phases (E.g. Project
       Planning, Requirements Analysis, etc.) have been eliminated or abridged as this work has already
       been completed under the 2023 Project.                                       
                                                                                    
       Phase 1: Bus Procurement and Build                                           
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
       Estimated Time to Complete: 18 months                                        
       Phase 1 CTE Fee Total: $72,000                                               
                                                                                    
       In this phase of the project, CityBus expects to purchase four hydrogen fuel cell electric buses from
       New Flyer that share the same specifications as those buses which the agency purchased via its
       2023 Low-No Project. Accordingly, the work required to finalize the bus configuration, technical
       specifications, and other documents required for bus procurement from New Flyer is expected to
                                                                                    
       be minimal.                                                                  
                                                                                    
       Prior to the contract award to New Flyer, CTE will provide a Pre-Award Buy America Audit. CityBus
       will execute procurement contracts with New Flyer. New Flyer will submit its final bus design and
       specifications document for CityBus approval before proceeding with production. CityBus and CTE
       will participate in a pre-production meeting either virtually or at New Flyer’s facility to confirm the
       design and to review quality control, quality assurance, and production procedures that will be in
                                                                                    
       place for this order. Once the buses enter the production line, CityBus and CTE will meet with New
       Flyer regularly during the bus build to track progress. The phase will end with the delivery of the
       buses.                                                                       
                                                                                    
       CTE will provide periodic inspection services. The goal of the inspection services is to ensure that
       the buses meet CityBus’s specifications and that New Flyer is adhering to its quality control and

       After delivery, CTE will conduct a Post-Delivery Buy America Audit to ensure the FCEBs meet Buy

       Phase 2: Infrastructure Design, Build, and Commissioning                     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
       Estimated Time to Complete: 12 months

       This phase of the project includes all necessary steps to design, build, and commission the hydrogen
       fueling station and to perform upgrades to maintenance facilities required for the safe indoor
                                                                                    
       storage and maintenance of FCEBs.                                            
                                                                                    
       CTE will assist CityBus as it finalizes the specifications for the hydrogen station, including

                     CEO REPORT  TO GLPTC  BOARD  OF DIRECTORS                      
                                                                                    
                                MEETING DATE: June 25, 2025                         
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          CEO                                                                       
          Hard to believe, but it is time to start working on next year’s budget. Joanne, Bryan, and I have
          been working on getting a draft together, with the goal of presenting the first look to the Board in
          July. We have been working with Purdue for off campus access, and with our contracted
          apartment contracts as part of the budgeting effort. We are also working on getting the best
          possible forecast for the effects of the property tax reform bill passed this year and possible
          alternative sources of funds.                                             
                                                                                    
          We are working with CTE again on submitting a grant application for the 2025 Bus&Bus
          Facilities/Low&No Emission programs.                                      
                                                                                    
          I will be traveling to San Francisco at the end of the month to attend the APTA Legislative
          Committee to advocate for the creation of a Transit Intensive Cities program for agencies with
          UZA (urbanized areas) between 200,000 and 1M. This is an effort to get the recommendation
          for the next reauthorization (current 5-year bill ends in September 2026). With local predictions
          that our UZA will exceed 200,000 in the next census, this could help us not lose 1/3 of our
          federal funding when that occurs. While this would happen sometime in 2032-33, now is the
          time to ask for the change.                                               
                                                                                    
          COO                                                                       
          Internal and public engagement around the proposed Reimagine CityBus network redesign
          remained a major focus throughout the month. Preparing for this effort has required strong
          coordination across departments, including the development of outreach materials, website
          updates, staffing of public meetings, and staff information sessions. These efforts have made it
          possible to connect with riders and stakeholders across the service area. The input we’ve
          received will be especially valuable as we roll out the updated network and make future
          adjustments based on real-world needs and rider experience.               
                                                                                    
          We continue to strengthen existing contract relationships and explore new partnership
          opportunities that support our express route services. These efforts are aimed at sustaining
          service and ensuring continued revenue support for this important segment of our operations.
          We also began transitioning demand response dispatching into the fixed-route dispatch office,
          consolidating all service coordination into a centralized control center. This operational shift is
          designed to streamline communication, improve efficiency, and support a more unified service
          delivery model.                                                           
                                                                                    
          Planning and training continue as we move toward implementation of the updated network, fare
          adjustments, and related policy changes in the coming months. Other ongoing projects,
          including the hydrogen fuel cell bus initiative, continue to advance as anticipated.
