          Mr. Adam Bradley gave a brief presentation on how CityBus has been using a simulator
          at Purdue, with big thanks to board member Mr. Tino Atisso for getting us set up with
          this. The simulator has pre-programmed scenarios which have been very helpful both
          for veteran operators and our new hires. This has helped our team develop situational
          and decision-making skills.                                               
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Ms. Julie Ginn made the motion to approve the CEO to sign contract with Mulhaupts for
                                                                                    
          the Fire Panel Procurement pending final legal review. Mr. Ben Murray seconded the
          motion. The motion was carried by a vote of 4 ayes and 0 nays.            
          CityBus is needing to upgrade our fire panel for the new hydrogen fueling station. The
          fire panel we currently have is beyond it’s useful life and cannot connect to cell, that is
                                                                                    
          how old it is. When looking at all of our systems, we decided we will update every
          system, which are through different companies and panels, and combine them into one
          single system. DLZ helped with the procurement and made sure that the project
          submitted stays within our budget range for this project.                 
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Ms. Julie Ginn made the motion to approve for the CEO to sign contract with Lesman
          Instrument Company for the H2 Monitoring System Upgrades Procurement pending
          final legal review. Mr. Tino Atisso seconded the motion. The motion carried by a vote of
          5 ayes and 0 nays.                                                        
                                                                                    
          DLZ also assisted in this procurement, making sure the submitted and accepted
          proposal stays within our budget range for this project. Our current monitoring system
          does not currently detect Hydrogen, which is why we are needing to make this
          upgrade. Some of our systems are currently working but past their useful life, so those
          are being replaced. CityBus has been in constant communication with the fire
                                                                                    
          department to be sure our upgrades are up to code.                        
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Mr. Jacques Vanier made the motion to approve the August 2026 Route Modifications.
          Ms. Julie Ginn seconded the motion. The motion was carried by a vote of 5 ayes and 0
          nays.                                                                     
          CityBus is changing the 32 Schuyler Ave to go back to what 2a 2b used to be, which will
                                                                                    
          now be called the 32A Schuyler Ave route and 32B Union Street route and will have one
          single bus on both routes operating at a one-hour frequency. The 35 Park East route
          will be removed from service, but the majority of the route will be serviced by Route 12
          South Street. The stops on Park East will be on demand and will operate at a 30-minute
          frequency.                                                                
                                                                                    
          Route 36 South 18th Street is also being removed from service. We will still service Ivy
          Tech to Walmart, but it will be serviced on Route 14 Brady Lane. Routes 13 and 14 will
          be modified for the first two runs of the day to assist in servicing major manufacturers.
          We will be starting at 5am for routes 11, 12, 13, and 14. Route 41 Connector is going
                                                                                    
          down to one bus, and the 21 McCormick Road will be getting rid of the 3rd bus during
          peak service.                                                             
                                                                                    
          GLPTC August Board Meeting Packet Pg. 3                                   
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Route 34 Teal Road, based on operations and public feedback, will have a slight
          modification, going all the way to 18th street and then rejoining back on State St. This
          will allow the bus to go to the front of the high school and also get close to the
          fairgrounds and all of those stores and Dr. offices in that area.         
                                                                                    
          There is roughly around $530,000 savings from all of this change. This number is not
          exact but an estimate on around what we could expect to save.

                    WHEREAS, GLPTC desires to program $82,336 from FFY 2024 5307 funds

     DATE:     08/14/2026                                                           
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     RE:       Bulk Discounts                                                       
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     With the recent changes to our fare structure and pricing, we have received numerous inquiries from
                                                                                    
     agencies, camps, and schools regarding bulk discounts for monthly and day pass purchases. We
     believe these requests are reasonable and represent an opportunity to better support the
     organizations we partner with and serve throughout our community.              
                                                                                    
     To address this need, we are recommending the implementation of a tiered discount structure based
     on purchase volume. This approach would provide greater affordability for our community partners
     while encouraging larger pass purchases and increased transit utilization.     
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
     Our recommended discount structure is outlined below:                          
                                                                                    
     31-Day Pass Bulk Discount:                                                     
                                                                                    
     Amount:   Discount % 31-Day   31-Day E&D                                       
     1 - 9     0%        $50.00    $25.00

          Introduction                                                              
          CityBus has secured an Innovative Finance and Asset Concession (IFAC) grant to advance an asset
          concession study centered on the CityBus Transit Center, the 218 Ferry Street site, and opportunity
                                                                                    
          areas along the proposed Lafayette Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor. The effort reflects CityBus’s
          need to pair transit investment with a practical strategy for real estate redevelopment, value
          capture, and long-term financial sustainability. As outlined in the IFAC work plan, the project is
          intended to evaluate underutilized public assets, identify Transit Oriented Development (TOD)
          and affordable housing opportunities, assess concession and revenue options, and prepare a clear
          implementation roadmap that links these strategies to BRT delivery. CityBus therefore needs a
          technical partner that can move beyond high-level concepts and provide rigorous market analysis,
          parcel and station-area assessment, financial modeling, and implementation guidance. Build
          America Center (BAC) at the University of Maryland is well positioned to meet this need by
          helping CityBus translate the IFAC award into actionable strategies that strengthen both the BRT
          corridor and the agency’s long-term institutional capacity.               
                                                                                    
          The Build America Center brings nationally recognized expertise in IFAC-related technical
          assistance, innovative finance, asset concessions, value capture analysis, and public-private
          partnership structuring. BAC’s work for the U.S. Department of Transportation and related federal
          programs has focused on helping public agencies evaluate transportation assets, identify revenue
                                                                                    
          opportunities, and structure financially sound pathways for implementation. This experience is
          directly relevant to CityBus’s needs under Tasks 2, 3, 4, and 7, including asset scan and market
          assessment, evaluation of concession and value capture options, P3 and innovative finance
          structuring, and development of an implementation roadmap. BAC also offers strong supporting
          capabilities for stakeholder, policy, and business planning tasks by linking transit, land use, and
          financing considerations into one integrated framework. Drawing on experience in value capture
          tools, TIFIA-readiness analysis, TOD strategy, and procurement planning, BAC can help CityBus
          assess which mechanisms are realistic, scalable, and aligned with local market conditions and
          governance capacity.                                                      
                                                                                    
          BAC is committed to serving as a hands-on, collaborative partner to CityBus throughout
          implementation of the IFAC grant. Our approach will emphasize technical rigor, practical decision
          support, and sustained knowledge transfer so that CityBus staff and partners can build internal
          capacity while advancing near-term project goals. BAC will lead the analytical work needed to
          evaluate redevelopment potential and financial feasibility, while coordinating closely with
          CityBus leadership and local stakeholders to ensure that recommendations are grounded in
          community priorities, institutional realities, and project delivery needs. In supporting Tasks 5 and
                                                                                    
          6, BAC will also help connect legal readiness, stakeholder engagement, corridor planning, and
          business strategy to the financial and asset-based analyses, ensuring that the final
          recommendations are not only innovative but implementable. Through this partnership, BAC will
          help CityBus produce a credible, phased strategy for asset monetization, TOD advancement, and
          BRT-supportive development that positions the agency for future funding, financing, and delivery
          opportunities.

          Objective and Tasks                                                       
          The objective of BAC’s study is to help CityBus translate its IFAC award into a practical, finance-
          ready strategy for leveraging public assets and advancing the Lafayette BRT corridor through
                                                                                    
          innovative financing, value capture, and transit-oriented development. BAC will lead the technical
          analysis needed to assess redevelopment and concession opportunities at the 218 Ferry Street site
          and other priority locations along the corridor, evaluate the feasibility and scalability of tools such
          as joint development, tax increment financing, impact fees, special assessment districts, naming
          rights, and long-term leases, and develop financial and implementation frameworks that align asset
          monetization with broader BRT planning goals. The study will identify market-supported
          opportunities, structure realistic revenue and partnership scenarios, and position CityBus for future
          funding and credit opportunities, including potential TIFIA and RRIF eligibility. Ultimately,
          BAC’s work will provide CityBus with an actionable roadmap to strengthen financial
          sustainability, institutional readiness, and community-centered corridor development.
                                                                                    
                                                                                    
          Task 1: Asset Scan, Value Capture, and Readiness Assessment (IFAC Task 2 & 3)
          BAC will lead Task 1 by conducting a comprehensive asset scan and value capture assessment for
          the 218 Ferry Street site, the CityBus Transit Center and Amtrak station area, and other priority
          locations along the proposed Lafayette BRT corridor. This work will build directly on the IFAC
          scope for Task 2 and Task 3 by identifying underutilized public assets and adjacent opportunity
                                                                                    
          sites, assessing market conditions and redevelopment feasibility, and evaluating how transit-
          oriented development, affordable housing, and related land uses can be integrated with corridor
          investment. BAC will assess the viability of a range of asset concession and value capture
          mechanisms, including long-term ground leases, joint development, air rights, tax increment
          financing, special assessment districts, impact fees, naming rights, and other revenue-generating
          strategies, with attention to financial feasibility, scalability, risk allocation, and fit with local
          market conditions and long-term transit needs.                            
                                                                                    
          In parallel, BAC will review enabling legislation, zoning requirements, and institutional capacity
          to identify barriers and opportunities for implementation and to support the development of
          governance structures and policy readiness necessary for successful asset redevelopment and value
          capture initiatives. The task will produce a set of practical, decision-oriented outputs that inform
          later financial structuring and implementation activities, including an asset scan and market
          assessment, a comparative value capture analysis, site and station-area mapping products, and a
          readiness assessment that highlights regulatory, institutional, and policy actions needed to move
          forward. BAC will also synthesize findings into recommendations for priority sites, preferred
                                                                                    
          mechanisms, and next steps that can be carried into finance structuring, stakeholder coordination,
          and the broader implementation roadmap. Relevant BAC experience includes the MCDOT Flash
          BRT Asset Scan, MCDOT Flash BRT Value Capture Analysis, MCDOT Flash BRT Value Capture
          StoryMap, which demonstrate the value capture analysis for the BRT project in Montgomery
          County in Maryland. Key Deliverables for this Task include:
